VAE: learn to reconstruct and to sample

An interactive field guide

A VAE replaces a single encoder output with a distribution. This creates a trainable connection between reconstruction and sampling from a prior.

Before this article: Autoencoders · Probability and tensors

What was already possible

Section titled “What was already possible”

An ordinary AE learns where each input should go, but it does not give a simple rule for drawing new codes. A latent-variable model instead defines p(z) and pθ(x|z). Its image density integrates over all possible z. For a nonlinear decoder, this integral and the true posterior pθ(z|x) are usually difficult to compute.

What changes, and why

Section titled “What changes, and why”

The encoder qφ(z|x) approximates the true posterior. It is not the prior and need not equal the true posterior. AEVB jointly learns this inference network and the decoder using a variational lower bound, or ELBO. Reparameterization gives a practical gradient estimator. These are the central contributions, rather than merely adding noise to an AE.

Posterior network

Two outputs parameterize qφ. They are not two separate latent samples.

mu, logvar = encoder(x).chunk(2, dim=-1)

Reparameterize

Noise is independent of encoder parameters. Gradients flow through the affine transform.

std = exp(0.5 * logvar)
z = mu + std * randn_like(std)

x_params = decoder(z)

The encoder approximates a posterior

Section titled “The encoder approximates a posterior”

A VAE defines a generative model p(z)p(x|z). Its encoder q(z|x) approximates the posterior over possible causes z of an observed image. The decoder is a likelihood model, not only a deterministic inverse. The prior describes codes before seeing the image, while the posterior describes codes after seeing it. Confusing these distributions makes the KL term hard to interpret.

The ELBO balances image explanation against departure from the prior. Maximizing it is equivalent to minimizing negative expected log likelihood plus KL. Pixel MSE appears only after choosing an appropriate Gaussian observation model and scale; it is not the unique VAE reconstruction term.

Work through the mechanism

Section titled “Work through the mechanism”

For a diagonal Gaussian, the network emits μ and log σ². Compute σ=exp(0.5 log σ²), draw ε from N(0,I), and form z=μ+σε. Randomness now enters through ε; the transformation from μ and σ remains differentiable. With μ=1, σ=0.5, and ε=0.5, z=1.25.

The demo shows a one-dimensional posterior and a standard-normal prior. KL is zero at μ=0 and σ=1. Narrowing σ does not always help: an extremely narrow posterior pays a large KL cost even if its mean is zero.

Move randomness out of the learned transformation

Section titled “Move randomness out of the learned transformation”

Directly treating a draw from q as an opaque operation hides how its distribution changes with encoder parameters. Reparameterization samples fixed standard-normal noise and transforms it with learned mean and scale. For a fixed noise draw, the path from encoder parameters to decoder loss is differentiable. The sample remains random across draws.

If μ=1, σ=0.5 and ε=−2, then z=0. A different ε gives another plausible code. In evaluation, decoding μ is deterministic but is not the same estimator as averaging stochastic decoded samples. Record which reconstruction protocol is used.

Core derivation

Section titled “Core derivation”

Insert q(z|x) into the latent integral. Jensen’s inequality moves log inside the expectation and produces a lower bound. This assumes q covers the relevant support.

Training is not sampling

Section titled “Training is not sampling”

Maximize expected log pθ(x|z) minus KL(qφ(z|x)||p(z)), or minimize its negative. Reconstruction evaluates samples from q; new generation draws z from p and does not use the encoder. Using μ alone for deterministic reconstruction is an evaluation choice, not the complete training procedure.

The loss reduction controls the effective KL weight

Section titled “The loss reduction controls the effective KL weight”

If reconstruction is summed over pixels while KL is summed over latent channels, changing resolution changes their relative scale. If one is averaged and the other summed, batch size can also alter behavior. Write reductions explicitly and report any β multiplier. Monitor reconstruction and KL separately: a low total loss can conceal an encoder that conveys little information.

What this does not establish

Section titled “What this does not establish”

KL regularization does not guarantee that every latent region produces a good image. A strong decoder may ignore z, called posterior collapse. Pixel likelihood, latent dimension, and KL weight all matter. A β other than one changes the standard ELBO objective.

Check your understanding

Section titled “Check your understanding”
Where does the encoder appear when generating a new image from the prior?

It does not appear. Draw z from p(z), then use pθ(x|z). The encoder is needed for inference from an observed x.
