EOSTok: make prediction preserve the image

An interactive field guide

An easier token prediction problem is not always a better image model. The tokenizer could simply discard information.

Before this article: TiTok · Attention and autoregression · Semantic representations

What was already possible

Section titled “What was already possible”

Two-stage systems train the tokenizer first, then train an autoregressive prior over fixed codes. End-to-end training could coordinate them, but a next-token loss alone rewards predictability. A collapsing or smaller effective vocabulary can lower prediction difficulty while harming image fidelity.

What changes, and why

Section titled “What changes, and why”

EOSTok jointly trains a 1D semantic tokenizer and causal image generator. IBQ uses softmax/straight-through assignments to connect gradients through quantization. Autoregressive Prediction Reconstruction, or APR, decodes teacher-forced predicted tokens to pixels and applies L2 plus LPIPS. DINOv2 alignment targets spatial hidden features, rather than forcing global latent slots to be pixel locations.

Read from left to right. Select a module to connect its role, tensor shape, and calculation. Snippets are teaching pseudocode; linked sources show the implementation.

Differentiable assignments

IBQ connects encoder, codebook, and generator gradients.

assignment = softmax(code_logits)
zq = straight_through_assignment(assignment) @ codebook

Teacher-forced prediction

Input embeddings can be formed from assignment probabilities.

inputs = assignment @ token_embeddings
predicted = causal_generator(inputs[:,:-1])

APR pixel supervision

Keep context assumptions visible when interpreting gradients.

prediction_image = decoder(predicted_codes)
loss_apr = l2(prediction_image,image)+lpips(prediction_image,image)

Work through the mechanism

Section titled “Work through the mechanism”

The demo uses two codes that decode to scalar values 0 and 2. A soft probability p gives expected output 2p. Pixel error changes with the predicted probability and produces a gradient. This isolates why reconstruction can supervise a predicted code distribution. It is a teaching relaxation, not the full IBQ estimator or free-running APR training.

Teacher forcing supplies true previous tokens when predicting the next one. APR therefore evaluates predictions under those contexts. It is not an unbiased gradient through a complete freely sampled image trajectory. The distinction matters when interpreting what end-to-end training actually optimizes.

Training is not sampling

Section titled “Training is not sampling”

In the encoder, image patches interact bidirectionally; queries use causal interaction, can read all patches, and cannot be read back by patches. The tokenizer and generator use learned absolute positions for the 1D latent sequence. APR adds another decoder forward pass, while semantic alignment adds a pretrained teacher dependency.

What this does not establish

Section titled “What this does not establish”

Use the matched 50-epoch ablation below to see the failure of naive joint training. Higher AR accuracy is not sufficient: the easiest codes can give worse images. Main guided results also use AutoGuidance for some sizes, which must not be treated as ordinary CFG with identical cost.

Check your understanding

Section titled “Check your understanding”
Why can AR accuracy rise while image quality falls?

The tokenizer may make its codes less informative and easier to predict. Pixel reconstruction and generated-image metrics test information that token accuracy does not.
