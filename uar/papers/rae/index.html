RAE: decode a frozen representation

An interactive field guide

RAE reuses a strong visual encoder and learns a decoder for its features. Its reconstruction decoder is a single-pass ViT.

Before this article: Semantic representations · DiT · DINO series · CLIP and SigLIP series

What was already possible

Section titled “What was already possible”

A low-dimensional VAE learns its encoder mainly for reconstruction and regularization. Modern pretrained encoders already contain useful visual structure. RAE asks whether their features can serve as the image representation for diffusion generation, while keeping that pretrained structure intact.

Tools this method builds on

Section titled “Tools this method builds on”

Freezing parameters stops their updates. It does not necessarily stop derivatives with respect to network inputs. A frozen feature extractor used on a reconstruction may still need an active input-gradient path; an encoder used only to supply fixed targets may run without that path.

LPIPS is a learned perceptual distance: compare normalized pretrained features, weight channel differences, and aggregate across positions and feature levels. In a reconstruction loss its parameters can stay frozen while gradients still pass through it to the reconstructed image.

Adversarial training uses a discriminator that learns to distinguish real from reconstructed samples. The generator learns to fool it. PatchGAN returns local-region scores. Its realism constraint differs from matching each output pixel to the corresponding source pixel.

What changes, and why

Section titled “What changes, and why”

Freeze a DINOv2, SigLIP2, or MAE encoder. Train a ViT decoder to reconstruct RGB from its patch features. A second-stage DiT learns to generate those features. This separates three roles: feature extraction, feature-to-pixel reconstruction, and latent generation. The reconstruction decoder itself does not run diffusion.

A semantic encoder is not a narrow image codec

Section titled “A semantic encoder is not a narrow image codec”

A representation encoder was trained to preserve useful features, not to minimize a specified bit budget. RAE keeps that encoder frozen and learns a decoder that maps its patch features back to pixels. The decoder is a single-pass ViT. The diffusion Transformer appears in the later generation stage, so the autoencoder itself is not an iterative diffusion decoder.

The DINOv2-B example resizes a 256² input to 224² for a patch-14 encoder. This produces 16 × 16 patch features of width 768. CLS and register tokens are not the image grid. A patch-16 output decoder reconstructs 256² pixels. There are 196,608 latent scalars, compared with 4,096 in a 32 × 32 × 4 VAE latent. The shared phrase “256 tokens” hides this large difference.

Work through the mechanism

Section titled “Work through the mechanism”

In the DINOv2-B recipe, resize 256² input to 224² before the patch-14 encoder. The 16 × 16 feature grid has 768 channels. A patch-16 decoder returns 256² pixels. This is 196608 latent scalars, compared with 4096 in a 32 × 32 × 4 VAE grid. Equal Transformer token counts do not mean equal information budgets.

The schedule demo fixes 256 latent positions and varies channels C. It illustrates a dimension-dependent time shift relative to a 4096-scalar reference. At C=16 the dimensions match and the shift is identity. A time shift is one adaptation; it cannot by itself solve the modeling difficulty of a larger feature space.

Why train a decoder on imperfect features

Section titled “Why train a decoder on imperfect features”

An exact encoder feature lies on the encoder’s data manifold. A generated feature may be slightly off it. A decoder trained only on exact features can be brittle to such errors. Noise-augmented decoding trains the decoder on perturbed conditions so that nearby imperfect features still map to plausible images. This is robustness to a chosen perturbation model, not proof that arbitrary generated features are valid.

The encoder’s parameters remain unchanged. The decoder still learns through all its layers. For a fixed feature, increasing σ makes exact reconstruction harder but can improve tolerance to a generator’s errors. The reported noise ablation improves generation FID while slightly worsening reconstruction FID. Those are different goals, not a contradictory result.

High-dimensional latents change the denoising problem

Section titled “High-dimensional latents change the denoising problem”

Copying the low-dimensional VAE noise schedule is not automatically appropriate for wider semantic features. The paper adjusts generation design, including the denoiser’s capacity and a dimension-dependent time shift. In the displayed shift, d is the effective dimension used by the chosen convention and dref is its reference. It is not the number of tokens alone.

α=d/dref,t′=αt1+(α−1)t\alpha=\sqrt{d/d_{\rm ref}},\qquad t^\prime=\frac{\alpha t}{1+(\alpha-1)t}α=d/dref​​,t′=1+(α−1)tαt​

If α=2, then t=0.5 maps to 2/3 while both endpoints remain fixed. The derivative is α/[1+(α−1)t]², which is positive, so the mapping preserves time order. This explains how the sampling density moves. It does not derive the best schedule for every encoder; the dimension definition, noise convention and experimental validation still matter.

Training is not sampling

Section titled “Training is not sampling”

The paper decoder recipe uses L1, LPIPS, and an adversarial term. Noise-augmented decoder training makes it tolerate imperfect generated latents. In the reported ablation this improves generation while worsening exact reconstruction. The released code was later refactored: its noise distribution and optimizer recipe must be recorded separately from the paper.

Reproduce the interface before comparing scores

Section titled “Reproduce the interface before comparing scores”

Record the encoder checkpoint, resize and normalization, token selection, latent normalization and decoder patch size. The audited decoder uses fixed two-dimensional sin-cos positions. Stage-two RoPE belongs to another network. Verify image-to-grid ordering and that the frozen encoder has no optimizer state. Evaluate clean reconstruction separately from noisy-condition robustness.

The paper decoder recipe and the later released training configuration differ. The paper describes half-normal perturbation strengths; the audited code samples sigma uniformly in a range. Optimizer betas also differ. Select and name one recipe rather than merging their convenient parts. Include the encoder’s pretraining and the perceptual/discriminator dependencies when reporting total training resources.

What this does not establish

Section titled “What this does not establish”

RAE inherits encoder pretraining cost and semantic behavior. Its high-dimensional continuous values are not a compact discrete bitstream. The lowest rFID in the table does not correspond to the highest linear-probe accuracy. Also distinguish fixed 2D sine-cosine positions in the reconstruction decoder from RoPE in the later generative backbone.

Check your understanding

Section titled “Check your understanding”
Which encoder weights change during RAE decoder training?

None in the frozen-encoder recipe. Improvements in reconstruction come from learning the decoder, while encoder features remain fixed.
